Swimming with turtles in Barbados: what to expect

A first-timer's guide to snorkelling with sea turtles on the west coast of Barbados: where to go, what you'll see, and how to make the most of it.

By the Horizon crew · Updated November 2026

Swimming with sea turtles is one of the most popular things to do in Barbados, and for good reason. The calm, clear water on the island's west coast is home to turtles that feed close to the surface, so you don't need to be an experienced snorkeller to get close to them.

Where can you swim with turtles in Barbados?

Most turtle trips run along the west coast, between Holetown and Bridgetown, where the Caribbean Sea is calm and sheltered. Boat tours are the easiest way to do it, because the crew knows where the turtles feed and takes you straight there.

Our tours leave from Folkestone Marine Reserve in Holetown, a protected area that's also home to a shipwreck and coral reef, so you get both in one trip.

Which turtles will you see?

On our turtle stop, you'll usually meet hawksbill sea turtles, recognisable by their pointed, bird-like beak and beautiful patterned shells. They're used to the boats and often swim right up to snorkellers while the crew feeds them.

What a turtle tour looks like

  1. Board the boat and get fitted with a life jacket and snorkel gear.
  2. Turtle stop: the crew feeds the turtles while you snorkel alongside them.
  3. Shipwreck stop: snorkel over a wreck surrounded by hundreds of fish.
  4. Fun time: slide, swim and relax on the way back.

Our 2-hour turtle and shipwreck tour covers all of this from US$60 per adult.

Tips for swimming with turtles

  • Look, don't touch. Turtles are wild and protected. Give them space and let them come to you.
  • Use reef-safe sunscreen, and put it on well before you get in the water.
  • Not a strong swimmer? Wear a life jacket and float. You'll still get a great view.
  • Bring an underwater camera or a waterproof phone case.
  • Book a morning trip if you can. The water is often at its calmest.

Can kids and non-swimmers come?

Yes. Children love the turtles, and life jackets are provided for everyone. On a glass-bottom boat like ours, anyone who'd rather stay dry can watch the turtles and reef through the glass.
